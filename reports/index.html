The big picture

Nvidia's first earnings report since Jensen Huang's abrupt exit beat expectations across the board — $115.8 billion in revenue and guidance of $127.4-$132.6 billion for Q4 — which, along with Anthropic's heavily anticipated IPO pricing, gave Wall Street two reasons for optimism on AI. That optimism was overwhelmed by Fed governor Christopher Waller's call for a half-point rate hike in December: the S&P 500 fell 1.03% to 7,340.53, the Dow dropped 1.64% to 50,759.42, and breadth was ugly, with only 75 advancers against 427 decliners. Gold tumbled 2% to a 14-month low below $3,800 and copper hit a four-month low as the hawkish repricing hit havens and AI-linked commodities alike, even though the VIX eased to 24.64 and futures pointed higher into Thursday. Target's blowout quarter and a premarket rally in AMD, Broadcom and TSMC showed pockets of strength, but credit-market executives warned of a 'fear factor' building as higher-for-longer rates squeeze weaker borrowers.

Three things to know

  1. Nvidia's beat buys the AI trade time, but the board's refusal to reinstate Huang keeps leadership risk alive.
  2. Fed's Waller wants a half-point December hike — markets are now pricing more hikes, not cuts.
  3. Anthropic's IPO pricing today is the real test of AI valuations, not Nvidia's earnings.
